# Interview: Lena, parent sharing care across two homes

> **Fictional.** Written for the PRD Assistant demo. Lena and everyone she mentions are made up.

- **Participant:** Lena, 29, nurse working shifts
- **Household:** Lena and her six-year-old son Milo, who spends every other week with his father, Bas; Lena's mother often picks Milo up from school
- **Shopping:** quick shops between shifts, often with Milo along
- **Lists today:** a notes app on her phone, plus messages to Bas and her mother

**Interviewer:** What makes shopping complicated for you?

**Lena:** Milo has two homes. Some things need to be at both, like his toothpaste and the one cereal he'll actually eat, and some things travel with him. Bas and I each keep our own list, so we both buy the swimming goggles, or neither of us does.

**Interviewer:** Who would share a list with you?

**Lena:** Bas, for Milo's things. My mother, because she picks Milo up and often stops at the shop on the way. But I don't want Bas to see my whole shopping list. I'd want one list for Milo's things that the three of us share, and my own list that's just mine.

**Interviewer:** How do you add things today?

**Lena:** In the moment, or never. I notice we're out of milk while I'm holding Milo on one arm and the fridge door with the other. If adding an item takes more than a few seconds, or needs two hands, I've forgotten it by the time I put him down.

**Interviewer:** What would make adding things work in those moments?

**Lena:** Speed, more than anything: open it, type "milk", done. At home, with my hands full, I'd probably just say it out loud if I could. Not in the break room at the hospital, though, with everyone listening.

**Interviewer:** What happens when two of you shop on the same day?

**Lena:** My mother and I once both bought a big pack of nappies, back when Milo still needed them. We laughed about it, but it was money we didn't have. If she could see that I'd already got something, that wouldn't happen. A tick next to the item with who bought it would be enough.

**Interviewer:** What would make you stop using a list app?

**Lena:** If it got complicated. My shifts change every week, and I don't have time to learn a tool. And if Bas found it a hassle, he'd stop using it, and then the shared list is useless.

**Interviewer:** What would success look like?

**Lena:** Milo has his things at both homes without Bas and me texting about cereal.
