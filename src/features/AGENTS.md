# Browser features: rules for working here

## The chat and the PRD (`workspace/`, `chat/`, `prd/`)
- **One conversation, two panes:** `workspace/workspace.tsx` owns `useChat`. The PRD is built from the conversation, the latest `saveSection` result per section (`savedSections` in `src/lib/chat.ts`); nothing else changes it. "Start over" stops the answer, clears the messages, the error and the check-in, and brings the intro back.
- **"Start over" asks first** (slice 28, `prd/start-over.tsx`): in a small pop-over under its button, "Clear the chat and the PRD?" with "Clear both" and "Keep them", so the row above the PRD keeps its two buttons and never wraps. It's a native `popover="auto"`, its button the invoker (`popoverTarget`, toggling), which gives light dismiss, Escape and the anchor, as the source's (below); `position-area: bottom span-left` on every screen, so it ends where its button ends. Focus goes to "Keep them" on its `toggle` event, since React's `autoFocus` focuses on mount, not when it opens (React's docs; MDN, *Popover API*, read 2026-10-26 through Context7); the browser gives focus back to the button on Escape or "Keep them". "Clear both" hides it first, then clears, and the intro's heading takes focus.
- **The palette** (slice 28, `src/app/globals.css`, `docs/decisions.md`, Design, 2026-10-26): `--background`, `--surface` and `--page` (the panels), `--border` (lines, also `--bubble`), `--border-strong` (a control's edge), and three text tones: `foreground` for headings, bold words, names and buttons, `prose` (`text-prose`) for the chat's replies and the PRD's statements, `muted-foreground` for the rest. A reply's bold words and its tables' headers, and what the code objects to under a statement, are `text-foreground` inside the prose.
- **The check-in** sends "<latest section> looks right. Please draft <next>." as the visitor's message, where the next is the first section in the PRD's order not drafted yet. It counts as arrived once a save of that section comes after the message it sent; until then it gives way, while the assistant works, to that section's own "Drafting…", and comes back after a failed or empty answer.
- **What the PRD shows changing** (slice 13): "Drafting…" sits beside the heading of each section being drafted while the assistant works (`sectionsBeingDrafted` in `workspace/workspace.tsx`): the check-in's section from its message until it arrives, and any section whose save has started in the answer on its way, until a save of it in that answer comes through. A save that failed keeps it, since the assistant can try again: counting only a save in progress, it blinked off and on with each try while the database was down (found 2026-10-25). It's a sibling of the `<h3>`, not inside it, so the heading's and the region's names don't change, and it carries `.shimmer`. A section saved, or saved again with different statements (compared as JSON), lights up: a decorative `span.just-changed` behind it, remounted with a new `key` on each change so the glow restarts without touching the heading's focus, a white tint that rises and fades as a statement's glow does in the README's video (`--duration-highlight`, 1.13s), which runs with reduced motion too, since a glow doesn't move. The sections the PRD mounts with, such as those brought back after a reload, and a save that changes nothing, don't count (`useChanges` in `prd/prd-document.tsx`, React's "storing information from previous renders" pattern). The title line's count rolls (`RollingNumber`, `.roll` in `src/app/globals.css`): plain text at rest, so a text query still finds the whole line; during a change the old number leaves upwards and the new one comes in from below, each moving half a line as it fades and sharpens from a 2px blur, over 0.4s on the ease-out, unclipped: a full line's slide in a box clipped with `clip-path: inset(0)`, 0.27s on the ease-in-out, crossed in a few frames and cut both numbers in half, which looked choppy (found 2026-10-25). With reduced motion the old number is `display: none` and no `animationend` comes, so the rolling markup stays until the next change. jsdom has no `AnimationEvent`, so React listens for a vendor-prefixed event there; `tests/unit/animation-event.ts` defines one before React loads.
- **Desktop layout:** the body takes the viewport height only on pages that hold `[data-workspace]` (`lg:has-[[data-workspace]]:h-dvh` in `src/app/layout.tsx`, Tailwind's `has-*` variant), so the two panes scroll on their own and the transcripts page scrolls as before. On those pages the page is at most 1600px wide (`max-w-workspace`, from `--container-workspace` in `src/app/globals.css`). The chat is in front and the PRD a supporting column beside it (slice 27; `docs/decisions.md`, Design, 2026-10-22): the grid is `minmax(0,1fr) max(28rem,36%)`, so the PRD is 448px on a 1024px screen, where its text keeps about 45 characters a line, about 500 on a 1440px one, 559 at the page's full width, and the chat takes the rest (496, 859, 961). The PRD's page has 24px of padding a side there (`lg:px-6`), 40 when the panes stack and 20 on a phone. A saved section's heading row doesn't wrap: a long heading wraps its own words (`min-w-0`), and its copy button stays on its first line (`self-start mt-0.5`), since a wrapped row left a blank row where the button hides at rest.
- **The chat's column** (slice 27): the chat's text, the visitor's messages, the notices, the suggestion and the message box share one column, at most 46rem (`--container-chat`, near opencode's 744px), centred in the chat however wide it gets. The message box, the suggestion and the allocation notice are `chat-column` (`mx-auto`); the conversation's pane spans the whole chat, so its scrollbar stays at the chat's edge, and is `chat-column-pane`: it centres its children with its left padding, `max(0px, (100% - 46rem) / 2)`, whose percentage is of the chat's width as the box's centring is, and caps each child at 46rem. So the conversation starts where the box starts, and where the chat has room, its questions end where the box ends. No container query: MDN's pages disagree on whether `container-type: inline-size` also adds layout containment (read 2026-10-23 through Context7), and the padding needs none. The header, the main area's padding and the footer take 114px of the height. The header is 45px on every page, not only the workspace, so it doesn't change height when the intro gives way to the chat; the focused skip link fits inside it. `tests/e2e/layout.spec.ts` measures all of it.
- **The typeface** (slice 18): Inter, through `next/font/google` in `src/app/layout.tsx` (Next.js 16, `next/font`, read 2026-10-21 through Context7): its files are downloaded at build time and served from `/_next/static/media/`, so the visitor's browser never asks Google, and the build needs Google Fonts to be reachable. Its licence is the SIL Open Font License 1.1 (https://github.com/rsms/inter, `LICENSE.txt`, read 2026-10-21), which allows bundling it with software. `variable: "--font-inter"` on `<html>` puts it first in `--font-sans` (`src/app/globals.css`, in `@theme inline`, so the variable resolves where it's used: Tailwind, *Theme variables*, read 2026-10-21 through Context7), with the system fonts behind it in case it fails. Inter is variable, so 300 (the big headings, slice 28), 400, 500 and 600 come from one file. Against a jump, Next adds "Inter Fallback", `local(Arial)` resized to Inter's metrics, shown until Inter arrives. On Windows Chromium, with Inter held back, the transcripts page grew 20px of 7,216 when it arrived, against 72px with plain Arial, and the layout-shift score stayed near 0 in both, as the top of the page barely moves; so `tests/e2e/pages.spec.ts` checks the fallback's form, not a measured jump. Where Arial isn't installed (Linux, Android), the fallback is the system's font, unresized.
- **The chat's spacing:** 16px from a question to its answer, or to "Thinking", and 32px between turns (`docs/decisions.md`, Design, 2026-10-20), through `data-role` on the conversation's items: a question that got no answer stays 32px from the next one. Inside an answer, 8px between steps in a row and 16px between a step and text either way (`spaceAbove` in `chat/conversation.tsx`, opencode's rule in `packages/session-ui/src/timeline/projection.ts` at 50c5fce, read 2026-10-20), and 24px before a failure notice, in the answer and above the chat's alert.
- **The moving line** (slice 11): while an answer is on its way exactly one line moves, the latest step in progress, or "Thinking" (slice 20, below) at the answer's end whenever no step runs and no reply arrives. Text at the answer's end counts as arriving even once its part is `done`, and so does a thought's time, which comes just before its reply: a reply's steps come after its text, so trailing text means the answer is finishing, and a "Working…", as the line read then, that flashed there before the stream closed left the chat 48px off its end, so it stopped following when the message box grew (found 2026-10-20 by `tests/e2e/layout.spec.ts`). Nothing moves once the status leaves `submitted`/`streaming` (`activityOf` in `chat/conversation.tsx`). An answer keeps one "Thinking" line from its first step to its reply's words: while a step runs between two waits it's parked, out of sight with `visibility: hidden` and `aria-hidden`, in its place, so shown again it neither blinks nor starts its band over; removed for each step and added anew, it did both at every step (found 2026-10-25). The unit tests count only the moving lines in sight. The band is the one shared class `.shimmer` in `src/app/globals.css`, for slices 13, 15 and 20 to reuse: a gradient clipped to the text, only under `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference` and `forced-colors: none` and where `background-clip: text` is supported, so everywhere else the line is still and grey (MDN, `background-clip`, Accessibility, read 2026-10-20 through Context7). It is decoration: the screen reader's status line in `chat/chat.tsx` is unchanged. jsdom applies no media queries, so the unit tests check which line carries `.shimmer`, and `tests/e2e/chat.spec.ts` checks the animation and the still line with `page.emulateMedia({ reducedMotion })`.
- **The search lines** (slice 15, `chat/search-step.tsx`, `chat/search-run.tsx`): opencode’s form at 50c5fce (`docs/decisions.md`, Design, 2026-10-20). Each search is a plain `<p>`, never a button: "Search" in medium weight, the query and what came of it in grey, 13px, one line cut off with `truncate` (a failure keeps its whole reason). The dot is `aria-hidden` and an `sr-only` "passages from" goes before the names. Searches in a row (`laidOut` in `chat/conversation.tsx`) fold into `SearchRun`: a button "Used 3 searches" with `aria-expanded`, whose count is the shared `RollingNumber` (`src/components/rolling-number.tsx`, also the PRD’s), then an inline SVG triangle, `-rotate-90` and `opacity-45` at rest, full on hover and turned down when open, through Tailwind’s `group-aria-expanded:`, turning in 0.4s on the ease-in-out as the lines slide (slice 26); its lines sit in `.fold` (`src/app/globals.css`), a one-row grid from `0fr` to `1fr` with `visibility` held back until the slide ends, and `inert` while closed, which takes them out of the accessibility tree and the tab order (MDN, `inert`, read 2026-10-20 through Context7). The box that clips them is positioned: the lines' `sr-only` labels are `absolute`, and placed against the chat's pane they escaped the clip and stretched the pane below its last line while a folded line was the answer's last (found 2026-10-22). Interpolating the row from `0fr` was checked in Chromium only (2026-10-20); where it doesn’t interpolate, the lines appear at once. Rows are `leading-7`, 8px apart, 4px under the button. While a run’s search runs, the run’s span carries `.shimmer`, and its white parts drop their colour, since `text-foreground` would cover the band. Its count, while it rolls, keeps its own grey (`.shimmer .roll`): the band paints the line through its see-through letters, and left out the rolling number, in a box of its own, so the count vanished for each roll (found 2026-10-25). The fake model searches three times when a message holds `FAKE_SEARCH_AGAIN`, which the end-to-end tests use. A run takes in the thought lines (below) in its unbroken stretch of searches and thoughts, before, between and after the searches, as opencode's `groupContent` does; a stretch with one search stays plain lines, and the count counts searches only.
- **Searches drawing together** (slice 26, `chat/search-run.tsx`): when a second search joins plain lines that were showing, in an answer on its way, a copy of those lines (`aria-hidden`) stays in the run's place, in its flow, so nothing around it moves, and in a layout effect, before the paint, the run measures it: the copy's lines move up into the first one's place as they blur out and fade (`.fold-leaves`, `--fold-y`) while their box closes to the folded line's height (`.fold-closes`, `--fold-from` to `--fold-to`), in 0.53s on the ease-in-out; from halfway "Used 2 searches", over them until the copy is gone (`absolute`), settles in, growing from 96% from its left (`.fold-settles`). The box's `animationend` removes the copy. Which lines were showing comes from `AssistantMessage`, which keeps how many parts showed before the latest came (`counted`): two searches that arrive together, a run an answer was brought back with, and reduced motion or a browser without `getAnimations` (`useMotion`) fold at once, as before. Once a run has formed, later searches only roll its count.
- **How long the model thought** (slice 20, `chat/thoughts.ts`, `chat/thought-line.tsx`): opencode's form at 50c5fce (`docs/decisions.md`, Design, 2026-10-21): "Thinking" while it thinks, 13px medium on a 16px line with `.shimmer`, then "Thought" in medium weight and white with the time in grey and tabular figures, as "Thought 8s", and from a minute "1m 5s", whole seconds rounded (opencode's `AssistantReasoningContent` in `packages/session-ui/src/message/message-content.tsx` and its words in `packages/ui/src/i18n/en.ts`, read 2026-10-21). The line doesn't open. The server can't time the reasoning, since the chat model's replies arrive whole, so the browser times each wait for a reply: `TimedTransport` in `workspace/workspace.tsx` pipes each answer's stream through `timeThoughts`, which starts the first wait at the request and each later one when the last step running finishes (a tool output), and puts a `data-thought` chunk with `{ ms }` just before the chunk that begins the next reply (text or a tool's input). The AI SDK keeps it as a part of the answer in that place (any `data-*` chunk without `transient`, ai 7.0.143, `processUIMessageStream`), so it's stored and restored with the conversation; a wait the answer ends in, stopped or failed, gets none. The parts go back to the server with the conversation, where `safeValidateUIMessages` accepts any `data-*` part and `convertToModelMessages` drops it unless given `convertDataPart`, so the model's input doesn't change (`tests/unit/chat.test.ts` compares it with and without the times). `finishedOnly` counts a thought as nothing finished. An answer kept before slice 20 has none and shows none, so `STORED_FORMAT` stayed 2: the new part only adds to what fits.
- **A reply is typed out** (2026-10-25, `chat/paced-text.ts`, `chat/rich-text.tsx`, replacing slice 26's words, which flowed in over half a second, every paragraph at once): opencode's way of showing a streaming reply (`docs/decisions.md`, Design, 2026-10-25). `usePacedLength` counts how many of the text's shown characters show: from none when an arriving text mounts, a step every 24ms, about 15 characters (600 a second) or more for a long text, so it's all shown within about 2.5 seconds of growing, each step ending just after a word that ends within 8 characters (opencode's snap). It paces the text as shown, after `parseMarkdown`, so formatting is in place as it types and a half-typed mark never appears; `RichText` lays out only the characters that show (`Words.shown`, `unreached`): a block, a list's item or a table's row that isn't reached yet isn't rendered, so a table grows row by row, its header first, its cells filling in. The words each step adds fade in together, one span a run (`Arrival`, `.word-arrive`: opacity over 0.18s on the ease-out, opencode's `markdown-word-enter`), and a list item that comes with a step has its bullet or number fade in with it (`.marker-arrive`). Only an answer that came in after the conversation mounted is typed out (`shownAtFirst` in `chat/conversation.tsx`), never one it mounted with, such as a conversation brought back after a reload; this, not the chat's status, decides it, and the typing can go on after the answer has ended. `RichText` keeps where each step's words begin (`starts`, in characters shown, React's "storing information from previous renders" pattern), so a step's fade isn't cut short by the next, and words shown before never fade again. Once a word's animation ends, the text looks in the next frame whether `getAnimations` lists any word still on its way, once a frame however many ended in it; when none is, everything shown is plain again (`settled`), one text node per run, as `getByText` and screen readers read it. Where motion is reduced or the browser lists no animations (`useMotion`, `chat/use-reduced-motion.ts`), the text shows whole at once, with no spans, so jsdom reads whole text; the unit tests of what moves define `getAnimations` themselves and use fake timers, moving them a frame at a time, since each step is set once the last has rendered. On a production build the long recorded reply typed out with no long animation frame (2026-10-25). The conversation stays out of any live region. axe reads a word mid-fade as low contrast, so `tests/e2e/accessibility.ts` finishes every animation that ends while axe reads.
- **Tables** (slice 19, `chat/rich-text.tsx`; the parser in `src/lib/markdown.ts`, which only the browser uses): opencode's at 50c5fce (`docs/decisions.md`, Design, 2026-10-20): a real `<table>`, its header's named cells `<th scope="col">` in medium weight over `border-border-strong`, rows over `border-border`, both `0.5px` (Chromium draws 1px on a screen of 1x), cells `p-3 align-top`. Each cell is `min-w-36`, which Chromium honours on table cells, so a wide table scrolls rather than squeezing its columns to a word per line. Every row is padded to the widest row or the header with empty `<td>`s, so the hairlines run the full width; a header's empty name is a `<td>` too, as it names nothing, and a row or header of nothing but empty cells is dropped by the parser. The table sits in `SideScroll`, an `overflow-x-auto` box that a `ResizeObserver` measures, on itself and on the table: while the table is wider, the box is `role="region"`, named "Table, scrolls sideways", with `tabIndex={0}`, so the keyboard reaches it (axe's `scrollable-region-focusable`, which checks only a box that overflows); a table that fits adds no tab stop. A hidden Browser pane delivers the observer's first measure only when it next draws. The cells' runs are built in `RichText`'s render, not inside `SideScroll`, so the fade's character count (`words.at`) keeps the rendered order. The fake model writes no table, so `tests/e2e/chat.spec.ts` opens a kept conversation that holds two.
- **Stop** (slice 12): while the assistant works, the message box's Send button is Stop, one `<button>` whose label and type change, so focus stays on it; an empty Send is `aria-disabled`, not `disabled`, which would drop focus to the page. A Stop click calls `preventDefault()`: `useChat`'s `stop()` can end the answer and turn the button back into a submit button before the browser's default action, which then sent the draft (found 2026-10-20 by `tests/e2e/chat.spec.ts`; jsdom's `fireEvent` doesn't show it). `stop()` aborts the request, sets the status to `ready` and calls `onFinish` with `isAbort` (ai 7.0.143, `AbstractChat.stop` and `makeRequest` in `node_modules/ai/dist/index.js`, read 2026-10-20; the AI SDK's *Stopping Streams*, through Context7); `afterAnswer` marks the question as stopped only when `stopRequested` says the visitor pressed Stop, since "Start over" aborts too. The route hands the cancel to the model call (`request.signal`), so no further model calls follow; the call in flight is a whole reply, which Cloudflare may still finish and count.
- **A stopped answer** keeps its parts as they were: its text so far, its finished searches, any section it saved (the PRD keeps it). `stopped` holds the ids of the questions whose answers were stopped, so an answer stopped before its first part still says "You stopped this answer." under its question. A step still running reads as stopped ("Stopped searching the interviews for “…”", "Stopped checking the quotes in …", without a full stop, like the finished step lines), never as running or failed. What goes back to the server is trimmed in the transport (`prepareSendMessagesRequest`, `finishedOnly` in `workspace/stopped-answer.ts`): unfinished steps are dropped, and so is an answer that finished nothing; the server's format check would refuse a half-arrived tool call, and the model would see a call without its result.
- **The message box grows** with its text through `field-sizing: content`, from three lines (`min-h-24`) to 180px (`max-h-45`), then scrolls; Chrome 123, Firefox 152 and Safari 26.2 support it (MDN's compatibility data, read 2026-10-20 through Context7), and older browsers keep the three lines. A `ResizeObserver` keeps the chat on its newest message when the conversation's pane gets shorter, as the box grows or the suggestion shows, unless the visitor scrolled up; jsdom has none, so `tests/unit/setup.ts` stubs it.
- **Scrolling panes are `relative`:** Tailwind's `sr-only` is `position: absolute`. Inside a scrolling pane that isn't positioned, those labels are placed against the page, stretch it and make the whole page scroll (found 2026-10-18). The PRD pane is also focusable (`tabIndex={0}`), or axe reports `scrollable-region-focusable` before it holds a link.
- **Jump to latest** (slice 14): shows while the chat is 48px or more off its end (`FOLLOW_MARGIN`), after a scroll up or when the conversation grows without one (a `ResizeObserver` on the conversation's `<ol>`, which changes only the button, not whether the chat follows; in Chromium, opening a folded line while the pane gains its scrollbar instead resizes the pane, and the pane's own observer takes the chat to its end). It's the scrolling pane's last child, a zero-height `sticky bottom-0` box whose `-mt-6` cancels the `space-y-6` margin it gives the item before it (Tailwind 4 puts that margin on every child but the last, read 2026-10-20 through Context7), with the button absolute inside; so the pane stays `#chat-title + div` with the same children, which `tests/e2e/layout.spec.ts` measures. `hidden lg:block`, since a phone's chat doesn't scroll on its own. A click sets following, moves focus to the message box (or the chat's heading once the allocation is used up and the box is gone) before the button unmounts, and calls `scrollTo` with `behavior: "smooth"`, or `"instant"` under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` (MDN, `Element.scrollTo`, read 2026-10-20 through Context7). While that smooth scroll runs, its downward steps don't count as the visitor leaving the end (`jumping`); a step upward does. If the conversation grows meanwhile, as opened searches still sliding open do, the conversation's observer scrolls on to the new end instead of showing the button; before that, the jump stopped at the old end and stayed there, with no button (found 2026-10-21, `tests/e2e/layout.spec.ts`, 2 runs in 60). It has an opaque background, as it sits over the text. jsdom lays nothing out and has no `matchMedia` or `scrollTo`, so `tests/unit/workspace.test.tsx` gives the pane a size, stubs both and reports the conversation's resizes itself.
- **Only "See it" scrolls the PRD.** The chat follows its newest message unless the visitor scrolled up; on a phone, where the panes stack and don't scroll on their own, it doesn't follow.

## Motion (slices 25 and 26)
- **One set of values** (`src/app/globals.css`, *Motion*), from the README's demo video (`docs/decisions.md`, Design, 2026-10-22; its project's `src/ui/motion.ts` and `src/Showcase.tsx`): its two eases; its three springs, sampled from Remotion 4.0.538's own `spring()` (`spring-utils.js` and `measure-spring.js` in the video project's `node_modules`) into `linear()` within 0.004, each as long as `measureSpring` says it takes to settle (0.4s, 0.5s, 0.77s); for each motion its fade, the video's `Math.min(1, p * k)` (1.4 for what arrives, 1.3 for a pane, 1.5 for the pop-over), as a `linear()` of its own, so things are whole while they still sharpen; and its durations, in its frames at 30 a second. The curves sit one a line under `/* prettier-ignore */`: to change one, sample it again rather than edit its stops. Without `linear()` (Chrome 113, Firefox 112, Safari 17.2, MDN's compatibility data) everything settles on the ease-out. Every motion in the app uses the set, Tailwind's own transitions too (`--default-transition-*` in `@theme inline`): the words, the fold's slide and its drawing together, the triangle's turn, the rolling count, the copy label and button, the just-changed glow and the pop-over.
- **What arrives** (`.arrive`) rises a few pixels, sharpens from a slight blur and fades in, then stays still: two animations, the motion on its curve and the opacity on its fade. The video's distances halved: a line 5px; a block 8px (`.arrive-block`: a table, a statement, the suggestion, the check-in, the failure and allocation notices); the visitor's message 13px and from 90% of its size, from its bottom-right corner, on the small spring (`.arrive-message`); the chat and the PRD 9px on the pane spring (`.arrive-pane`); a search 15px from the left, fading on its motion's own curve, as the video's (`.arrive-search`). `--order` delays one 0.2s after another (a section's statements; the PRD's pane after the chat's), and `data-again` starts an arrival again by switching to copies of its keyframes, so a re-saved section's statements arrive again without remounting a focused name. `--arrive-*` and `--order` are registered with `inherits: false` (`@property`, as Tailwind 4 registers its own): inherited, the chat pane's 9px and the PRD pane's delay passed to everything in them (found 2026-10-22). `backwards` holds the first frame through a delay, and nothing stays once it has run, no transform or filter to make a containing block. Only under `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`. A step that arrives while it works, a search or a quote check, also carries `.shimmer`, whose `animation` shorthand mixed with `.arrive`'s parts: the line rose in again every 0.4s for as long as it worked, with no band, which showed only when a step waited, as on a slow or failing database (found 2026-10-25). `.arrive.shimmer` lists the three animations side by side, the arrival once and the band on, `.arrive` sets its count to 1, and `tests/e2e/chat.spec.ts` holds a search and a save in progress to check it. Any other class that animates an element that may arrive needs the same.
- **Only what comes after the page showed:** the conversation's messages by `shownAtFirst` (`chat/conversation.tsx`), whose `arriving` reaches every line of an answer; the PRD's statements by `useChanges`; the rest by `useArrival` (`src/components/arrival.ts`): it arrives unless it was showing when its component mounted and has stayed since, and under a new name (the check-in moving to the next section) it arrives again. React's "storing information from previous renders" pattern, since `react-hooks/set-state-in-effect` and `react-hooks/refs` rule out an effect or a ref that marks the page as shown. A saved section's line is keyed by what it says (checking, stopped, failed, saved), so each is a new line that arrives; a search's line stays one line as it finishes. Not arriving: "Thinking" and "Drafting…", status lines that just appear, as the video's Thinking does; the folded line, which settles in only as plain lines draw into it (above). The intro arrives as the page first shows (below).
- **The tick** (`Tick` in `chat/section-step.tsx`, `.tick-draws`): the video's check at half size. On a line that arrives its circle draws from the top in 12 frames, the tick from frame 8 in 10, and one ring pulses out 7px from frame 10 in 18 (`::after`); dash lengths 41 and 9, the circle's and the tick's. Brought back after a reload, it's simply there.
- **The chat follows its end as laid out** (`followEnd` in `chat/chat.tsx`): its children's `offsetTop + offsetHeight`, which leave transforms out. Following `scrollHeight`, which counts a rising line's pixels while it rises, the chat went past its end, then the whole conversation sank back as the newest line settled, instead of the line rising into place (found 2026-10-22 in a recording; `tests/e2e/layout.spec.ts`). It showed only when an arrival was the chat's last line, as a failure notice is. Jump to latest still scrolls to `scrollHeight`, which the browser clamps. A reply being typed out grows after its answer has ended, when the layout effect on the messages and the status no longer runs, so `RichText` calls `onGrow` (`followTyping`) before each paint while it types; it left the chat a line short of its end in 2 runs of 8 (found 2026-10-25).
- **Room on the left:** the chat's scroller has 8px more padding on its left than its column needs (`--chat-room`, added to `chat-column-pane`'s padding), given back by an 8px negative margin (`lg:-ml-2 lg:[--chat-room:0.5rem]`), so the tick's ring and a search sliding in aren't cut off where it scrolls (a box that scrolls clips sideways too); nothing moves.
- **Tests:** jsdom runs no animation, so the unit tests check which elements carry the classes; the end-to-end tests check `animation-name` under both motion settings, that a settled element has no opacity, transform or filter left, and that a reload brings nothing in. What lasts under a second, or is gone once it has run (the words' spans, the lines that fold, the pop-over's transitions), they record as it comes, with `recordArrivals` (`tests/e2e/motion.ts`, a `MutationObserver` from an init script) or the transition and animation events. `tests/e2e/accessibility.ts` and the layout tests' `childGaps` finish every animation that ends before they read, and a test that measures the pop-over finishes its opening first.
- **Checking motion by recording it** (slice 25, 2026-10-22): the scenario tour on a production build (`NEXT_PUBLIC_CHAT_REPLAY=1 npm run build`, then `AI_CHAT_PROVIDER=replay REPLAY_SPEED=4 npm run start -- --port 3201`, the end-to-end replay server's settings at a watchable pace), driven by a Playwright script: `page.screencast.start({ onFrame })` hands over each frame the browser presents with its time; an init script's `MutationObserver` logs each arrival as it mounts, with its box then and 1.5s later; a `long-animation-frame` `PerformanceObserver` logs long frames. Frames around each arrival, cropped and laid out as a grid in a local HTML page, screenshotted by Playwright, show what moved. Judge long frames on a build only: the dev server's React is several times slower (on the build, none during the tour, two at load). Slice 26 recorded the tour the same way, then a source's pop-over opened, switched to another name and closed; the tour's longest answer, 840 words in the rich-text step, is the one to watch for long frames. A hidden Browser pane draws no frames. The build replaces `.next`, so stop any server on it first, and end the `next start` process a stopped command leaves on its port.

## After a reload (`workspace/stored-conversation.ts`)
- **What's kept:** the whole conversation, in `localStorage` under `prd-assistant:conversation`, with `format` (`STORED_FORMAT`, 2 since slice 12 added `stopped`), `interrupted` and `stopped`, the questions whose answers the visitor stopped. The PRD isn't stored apart: it's rebuilt from the conversation, as always, and its chat quotes find their messages there. `Workspace` keeps it after every answer, finished or failed, never while one is on its way, and forgets it once the conversation is empty ("Start over", a failed first request). A browser that refuses storage or has no room keeps nothing, and nothing else changes.
- **Browser-only rendering:** `use(browser())` from `react-dom` (React 19.3) inside a `<Suspense>` whose fallback is the intro without its handlers. The server's page is that intro; in the browser the workspace's first render already holds what's kept, so `useChat` starts with it (`messages`) and nothing flashes between hydration and the restored view (https://react.dev/reference/react-dom/browser and Next.js's *Single-Page Applications* guide, "Rendering components only in the browser", read 2026-10-20 through Context7). The fallback's buttons are `aria-disabled` until then: they look the same, and Playwright waits for them instead of clicking into nothing.
- **A kept answer that broke off** (`interrupted`) shows as failed after the reload, with "Try again", until the next request, exactly as before it; sending a new message drops it first, as after any failure.
- **The check:** `restoreConversation` checks the stored form with Zod, synchronously, before the first render. It checks to the letter what the browser reads (a saved section, a search's passages, each part's type and state) and lets the rest through loosely; the server checks every message again when the next one is sent. A conversation that doesn't fit is removed, and the intro says "The PRD you started earlier couldn't be brought back: the demo has changed since. You can start a new one." until the visitor goes on. The schemas are held to the types in `src/lib` with `satisfies`, so a change there fails the typecheck. **Raise `STORED_FORMAT`** with any change that could stop an older conversation fitting: `ChatMessage`, the checked section, the parts the chat shows, or an upgrade of `ai`. A new kind of part that older conversations simply lack, such as the thinking times, doesn't need it.
- **Kept out of `src/lib`,** like the intro's logic, so the measurement's comparison doesn't report it.

It copies `sectionMarkdown` from `prd/prd-markdown.ts`: the download's form for one section, footnotes numbered within it.

Tailwind 4 already puts `hover:` and `group-hover:` inside `@media (hover: hover)` (upgrade guide, read 2026-10-21 through Context7). It stays clickable while transparent, as opencode's does.
- **Its label** (`.copy-tip` in `src/app/globals.css`) is CSS, `aria-hidden`: shown on `.copy-button:hover` after 0.4s, closed by `data-pressed` from the press until the pointer leaves, held open by `data-open` to say "Couldn't copy". Not `role="status"`: the chat's status line is the page's one. `navigator.clipboard.writeText` rejects when refused (MDN, read 2026-10-21 through Context7), and a page that isn't a secure context has no `navigator.clipboard`; both read "Couldn't copy". The desktop app's Browser pane refuses the clipboard while it's hidden, so a successful copy is seen only in the end-to-end tests there (found 2026-10-21).

## The scenario tour (`workspace/scenario-tour.tsx`, slice 24)

It's a `fixed` `<aside>` over the page's corner, beside the intro or the workspace, never in their layout, so the layout tests measure nothing new.

## The intro (`workspace/intro.tsx`, `workspace/disclosure.ts`)
- **Its form** (slice 28, `docs/decisions.md`, Design, 2026-10-26): centred, at most 48rem wide; the heading in weight 300; one short line; "Interviewed:" and the six names, each a link to `/transcripts#<slug>`, the interview's anchor on the transcripts page; the buttons; then `Glimpse`, an inline SVG of the chat beside the PRD in bars, `aria-hidden`, faded at its foot (`mask-b-from-40%`, Tailwind 4.1's mask utilities), `hidden sm:block`. The notices (a discarded PRD, a failed first request, the allocation) are left-aligned boxes in the centred column. At 1280×720 the glimpse ends inside the window (`tests/e2e/intro.spec.ts`).
- **Its arrival:** its lines are `.arrive` with `--order` 0 to 4, the glimpse `arrive-pane` and last, in the server's page as in the browser's. The browser's intro replaces the server's a moment after the first paint, which would start every arrival again; so `useArrivalSinceFirstPaint`, in a layout effect, sets each of its animations' `startTime` to the `first-contentful-paint` entry's `startTime`, both on the document's timeline (MDN, `Animation.startTime`, `Document.timeline` and `PerformancePaintTiming`, read 2026-10-26 through Context7). They go on where the server's page had got to, or are over, and an intro brought back later is simply there. Without a paint entry they run from now. On the dev server the first paint can come seconds after the page is asked for, so a screenshot then catches the arrival at its start.
- **What shows when:** the intro until the visitor leaves it or the conversation holds messages (`conversationStarted`, which holds for a restored conversation too); the suggestion, "Add grocery delivery to the PRD.", once a section is saved, until the visitor has sent it (`offeredSuggestion`). Its button and the suggestion import their words from `src/lib/suggestions.ts`, never a copy: the measurement sends the intro's words too, and changing them means a new baseline. The suggestion itself isn't measured; the measurement's grocery delivery claim (`GROCERY_DELIVERY_CLAIM`) was the suggestion until 2026-10-22.
- **A failed first request:** `useChat`'s `onFinish` runs after every answer, once the error status is set, with `isError` and the messages from the chat's own state (ai 7.0.143, `makeRequestImpl` in `node_modules/ai/dist/index.js`, read 2026-10-19; the AI SDK's `useChat` reference through Context7). `afterAnswer` brings the intro back only when the intro's request failed before a section was saved, and clears the error, so the chat never opens on the old one. The server can send an error after a save (a reply cut off at its output limit, or the provider failing on the last call); then the section stays, with the chat's "Try again". Don't decide this in `onError`: it runs before the status is set, and the rendered `messages` can lag behind the chat's state. A ref marks the intro's request, so a later failure stays in the chat.
- **The message box's text lives in `Workspace`,** passed to `Chat`: the chat unmounts when the intro comes back, and what the visitor typed during a failed first request must survive. "Start over" leaves it, as before the intro.
- **Focus when the view changes:** `sendMessage` adds the message only after an await, so `flushSync` in the click can't place focus. The event names its target in a ref, and a layout effect keyed on the view focuses it once the new view has rendered: the chat's screen-reader heading after the button, the message box after the quiet way in, "Or ask your own question first", the intro's heading when it comes back. Not `useEffect`: for an update that isn't a click, such as a failed request, React runs a plain effect only after the browser paints, and until then focus sits on the page (React's `useEffect` caveats, read 2026-10-20 through Context7; found 2026-10-20 as a unit test that failed under load). The suggestion unmounts with the button that has focus, so its click focuses the message box before it sends.
- **Not a named region:** the intro's heading mentions a "shared grocery list app", and the PRD is the region of that name; Playwright matches names as substrings, so a named intro would make `getByRole("region", { name: "Shared grocery list app" })` ambiguous.
- **Kept out of `src/lib`:** the intro's logic lives here, not in `src/lib`, whose changes the measurement's comparison reports as code that shapes the model (`src/server/measurement/report.ts`).

## When the AI can't answer (`workspace/quota.tsx`)
- **The used-up allocation and the visitor's used-up day:** `useChat`'s `onError` (`noteDayOver`) sets `dayOver` from the error's message (`dayOverIn`): "quota" for `QUOTA_USED_UP`, the server's words for Cloudflare's quota 429, and "limit" for `DAILY_LIMIT_REACHED`, the chat route's 429 once the visitor has sent the day's messages, which the AI SDK makes the error's message (`createUIApiCallError` in `node_modules/ai/dist/index.js`); both are in `src/lib/chat.ts` (`src/server/AGENTS.md`, *The rate limit*). Both end at 00:00 UTC, so they share `QuotaNotice`, which words each its own way. It lasts until a reload, through "Start over" too. The intro then shows `QuotaNotice` instead of its button and "Or ask your own question first"; the chat shows it instead of the suggestion, the message box and "Try again"; the PRD drops its check-in, which sends a message too. The conversation, the PRD and the link to the interviews stay.
- **The reset time:** 00:00 UTC, and the same moment in the visitor's locale and time zone (`nextReset`, `localTime`), computed in the browser when the notice first shows, so the server's render never holds a time.
- **Busy:** an error whose message is `DEMO_BUSY` shows those words with "Try again", since a minute later it can work. Every other error keeps the generic sentence.

## The source pop-over (`prd/source-popover.tsx`)
- **No library:** one native `popover="auto"` element serves every name. It shares its look and its motion with "Start over"'s (`.popover` in `src/app/globals.css`; `.source-popover` adds its sizes and placement), and both grow from what opened them through `growFrom` (`src/components/popover.ts`). Each name is its invoker (`popoverTarget`, `popoverTargetAction="show"`), which gives light dismiss (a tap outside, Escape) and makes the name the pop-over's implicit anchor, so the CSS needs no `anchor-name` (MDN, *Using the Popover API*, "Popover anchor positioning", read 2026-10-18 through Context7).
- **Position** (`.source-popover` in `src/app/globals.css`): on a phone it isn't anchored, nearly full width and scrolling inside. From `lg` up it uses `position-area` with `position-try-fallbacks`, inside `@supports (position-area: bottom)`, with `inset` and `margin` reset as MDN advises, so browsers without anchor positioning get the centred box. Support (MDN's compatibility data, read 2026-10-18): Chrome 129, Firefox 147, Safari 26.
- **Highlighting** (`src/lib/highlight.ts`) reads a quote the way the check does, only to mark the words; whether a quote is confirmed is the check's call alone.
- **Its motion** (slice 26, `.source-popover` and `.quote-mark` in `src/app/globals.css`): it grows from 92% and fades in on the pop-over spring, with `@starting-style`, and closes the same way with `display` and `overlay` among the transitions (`transition-behavior: allow-discrete`), which keep it shown and on top until it's done; closing, it takes no clicks. Support (MDN's compatibility data, read 2026-10-22): `@starting-style` Chrome 117, Firefox 129, Safari 17.5; `overlay` Chrome 117 only, so the closing transition sits in `@supports (overlay: auto)` and elsewhere it closes at once (in Firefox `display` doesn't transition either). It grows from its name: `openFrom` in `prd/source-popover.tsx`, run by the name's click before the browser shows it, sets `--grow-from` in the next frame, before the paint, to the point of its box nearest the name, from `offsetLeft` and `offsetTop`, which leave out the scale (for the top layer's fixed box they're the viewport's, as `getBoundingClientRect` gives the name's, with the page scrolled too: Chromium, 2026-10-22). On a phone, unanchored, it grows from its edge nearest the name. Its passages remount at each opening (`opened`), and from 0.4s after it opens the highlight (`.quote-mark`, a gradient, not a background colour) sweeps across the words in 0.8s on the ease-in-out, as `background-size` grows, in reading order across its lines (`box-decoration-break: slice`, the default). On a phone the dimming behind it fades in and out with it.
- **A second name while it's open:** the browser keeps an open pop-over by the name that opened it, and a click on another of its invokers neither closes it nor shows it again, so the second name's quote showed by the first name (found 2026-10-22). `openFrom` shuts it at once first, without its closing transition, and takes in the closed state (`getBoundingClientRect`), so the name's own click opens it anew by itself, from `@starting-style`; it also finishes a closing transition still running.
